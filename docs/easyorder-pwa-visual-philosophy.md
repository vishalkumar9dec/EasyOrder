# Shared Table Systems

Shared Table Systems treats collaboration as a visible meal surface: not a chat thread, not a checkout funnel, but a composed table where every person leaves a small, legible trace. Space is arranged like a dinner table seen from above, with items, people, timing, and checkout pressure held in deliberate balance. The final work should feel meticulously crafted, as if every chip, quantity marker, price, and state transition was placed by someone with deep product and visual systems expertise.

Color is warm but not nostalgic: coriander green, tomato red, steel ink, coconut white, and packet yellow form a practical commerce palette. Food and supplies appear as distinct but compatible zones, giving the user an immediate sense of what comes from restaurants and what comes from quick commerce. This must be handled with painstaking attention, where color is an information system first and decoration second.

Scale communicates authority. The shared cart deserves the largest visual weight, guest activity lives as smaller orbiting signals, and checkout readiness sits as a firm, grounded rail. Rhythm comes from repeated item rows, avatar marks, price tags, and availability ticks. The composition should look like the product of countless refinements, with enough density to feel useful and enough air to feel calm on a phone.

Typography is restrained and functional, but never generic. Display moments can be compact, bold, and app-like; operational labels should be crisp, quiet, and fast to scan. Text is sparse inside the visual system: labels, amounts, ETAs, dietary tags, and action verbs. The design must feel master-level in its execution, with every word behaving like interface material rather than marketing copy.

The subtle reference is the Indian group-order ritual: people gathered around one decision, negotiating taste, budget, timing, and fairness. The interface should not literally draw a dining table, but its structure should make the group feel present. Every surface should suggest a carefully mapped social meal: host responsibility, guest agency, shared budget, and the final handoff to Swiggy handled with confident craft.
